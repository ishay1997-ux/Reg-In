# module-build — continue a REG-IN module build

The active module's `docs/micro_guides/module-N.md` is the approved plan and your memory — continue from its "Active step". This is normal build flow, NOT Plan Mode (iron rule 2): the micro-guide already IS the approved plan and its 🔻🤖/👤 stop-points are the control. Build directly; DB/RLS before UI.

## Gaps, unconfirmed instructions, and claim discipline

Four standing rules on what to do when the plan under you is incomplete, unverified, or
merely *clear*. **Everything here reports to Ishay** — there is no intermediate session.

- 🔴 **The gap protocol — never silent-fill.** The blueprint is not always detailed enough, and the
  failure mode is guessing quietly (Ishay's own words, 01/08: *"הבלופרינט לא תמיד מפורט מספיק עבורו
  ואז הוא מנחש לפעמים"*).
  🆕 🔴 **Before you classify anything as a gap — look in the approved spec.** It is **tier 2** and
  routinely answers exactly what the micro-guide left out. **It is a gap only after you looked there and
  the spec was silent** — and "the spec is silent" is a claim you must earn by having opened the files,
  not an impression from the guide. ⚠️ **And the set you searched must be the set `spec.md` §① names** —
  open that list and work it, do not work a shortlist restated from memory or from this file. **An
  absence-claim is only as wide as the places actually searched**, so a narrow search that reports
  "silent" is a wrong answer wearing the uniform of a checked one. **Why this step is first and not optional:** without it a weight, a
  threshold, a status label or a screen label that Ishay already ruled gets classified *technical*,
  decided again, and stamped `הכרעתי` **under Claude's name** — which reads to him as a fresh decision he
  never made, and quietly overrides a real one.
  **Then, and only then:** a **technical** gap ⇒ decide, mark it `הכרעתי, הפיך`, and report it. A gap
  with any **product flavour** ⇒ a question to Ishay. **Filling it silently is the one
  unacceptable option** — it turns a guess into an unreviewable fact.
- 🆕 🧭 **A clearly-written instruction is not a confirmed one.** The gap protocol above covers a spot
  that is **under-specified**; it produces no signal at all for one that is **perfectly clear and was
  never confirmed with Ishay** — you will simply build it well and wrong. ⇒ **An instruction that
  encodes process or product (timing · who is notified · what is permitted · what a status means ·
  what happens when X) and cites no source ⇒ ask Ishay whether he actually confirmed it**, before
  building. *(Ishay, 02/08: the spec itself can be wrong, and the save⇄send gap confused nobody —
  everyone understood it perfectly and built the wrong thing.)*
  🆕 🔴 **And here is the observable version, because "notice that it was never confirmed" is a feeling and not a trigger** *(added 08/08/2026 — a scan measured this rule at zero occurrences in every micro-guide: it has never once fired)*. **The check is a source lookup, not a judgement: does this instruction trace to tier 1–3 — `docs/schema.sql`, the approved spec, or the frozen C5/C6 — or does it exist ONLY in the micro-guide?** **Micro-guide-only ⇒ nobody ruled it; Claude wrote it.** ⇒ ask. **And the pass leaves a line either way**, in the same 🗣️ brief: *"הוראות שמקורן במדריך-המיקרו בלבד: … / אין"* — **without it, a session that ran the check and a session that skipped it produce identical output**, and Ishay cannot read the guide to tell them apart.
- **Tag every claim in your reports** as `אומת-על-ידי` (you measured it) · `דווח-לי` (someone told
  you) · or 🆕 **`הנחתי`** (you filled a gap yourself — nobody said it and you did not measure it).
  It is the מדדתי/על-דיווחו split Ishay expects in every report, and it is what lets a reader
  know where to dig. 🔴 **The third tag is the one he actually asked for** (07/08/2026, his words:
  *"לא תמיד ברור לי מה Claude הבין, מה הוא הניח, ואיפה הוא השלים פערים בעצמו"*). **Two tags cannot
  express an assumption** — an unmarked claim reads as verified, so a gap you filled silently is
  indistinguishable from a fact you measured.
  🆕 🔴 **And `הנחתי` — that one alone — also gets a line in the micro-guide's deviations log (locate it by heading NAME — the § number differs between guides), in the same session** *(Ishay's ruling 08/08/2026)*. **Why only the third:** the other two describe **how you learned** something, which matters to Ishay while the conversation is live; `הנחתי` describes **something you invented**, and that is what a session two weeks from now needs to find — because by then it reads exactly like a fact. **Measured, and it is why this ruling exists: all three tags occur ZERO times across `module-1/2/3.md`** — their only home was chat, so every assumption ever filled is now unrecoverable. That log already holds the `↳ as-built` lines; an assumption belongs in the same register. **The other two stay in chat by the same ruling — that is deliberate, not an oversight: their reader is Ishay in the moment, and writing them to disk adds a record with no reader.**
  ⚠️ **And this is provenance, not confidence: state
  *where it came from*, never a percentage** — verbalized confidence is measurably overconfident,
  and a number would give him the feeling of control without the control.
- 🆕 📌 **Ruled 08/08/2026, so a future scan does not re-open it: the two standing questions below, and the 3-attempt fix cap further down, stay CHAT-ONLY on purpose.** A scan measured them at zero occurrences in every micro-guide and flagged them as unauditable — correctly, but the conclusion does not follow. **The test this project uses: does a FUTURE session need it, or only Ishay in the moment?** These two are asked *of him*, and the 3-attempt cap *produces a stop he necessarily sees* — the audit is that he is in the conversation. **Writing them to disk would add a record with no reader**, which is the padding-as-thoroughness failure, not a fix. *(Contrast `הנחתי` and "מה ייחשב עובד" above, which a later session genuinely consumes — those went to disk under the same test.)*
- 🔴 **Two standing questions you owe Ishay, aimed at the prompt you were handed — not at your plan:**
  **① אילו מוקשים הפרומפט הזה לא זיהה? ② מה לא נבדק לפני שהפרומפט הזה נכתב?** These force you to
  investigate the prompt instead of receiving it. **"הכל בסדר" without having looked is the failure
  they exist to prevent** — a prompt is often built substantially on your own earlier reports, so its
  blind spots are yours too.

## Pre-flight
0. 🆕 🔴 **Resuming into a half-built unit — check the approval, do not inherit it.** The 🗣️ Plan-Mode gate below is enforced by the tool layer **within a session**, and a session boundary dissolves it: a session that starts fresh is simply not in Plan Mode, and nothing on disk says whether the unit in progress was ever approved. ⇒ **Before writing a line of code for a unit already underway, find its approval line in the micro-guide** (`🗣️ אושר DD/MM HH:MM` in the step table or the Decisions Ledger). **No line ⇒ treat the unit as UNAPPROVED: re-enter Plan Mode and put the brief to Ishay again.** *(Yes, that may re-ask something he already approved — that is the cheap error. The expensive one is code he never saw, reported as gated.)* 🔑 **And the other half of the same fix: when he approves, write that line to disk before you start coding** — an approval that lives only in chat is an approval the next session cannot find.
1. **Rule 16 — one writing session at a time.** Unsure if another session is writing? Ask Ishay before editing.
2. **Rule 10 — mid-session merge check.** Run `git fetch origin`, then `git merge-base --is-ancestor HEAD origin/dev`. If the branch is already merged into `origin/dev`, STOP — don't pile commits on a dead branch; tell Ishay and offer a fresh branch from `dev`. ⚠️ **Mandatory caveat before trusting the result: the check also succeeds on a freshly-cut branch with zero commits** — the discriminator is `git log origin/dev..HEAD --oneline` (empty ⇒ fresh branch, not merged). Full wording: iron rule 10 in root `CLAUDE.md`.
3. **Model.** Follow the "Model & effort per phase" table at the top of the micro-guide's **Phase & Step Plan section — locate it by its heading, not by number** *(corrected 25/08/2026: this line said "§5", but in every guide since module 4 the plan is §6 and §5 is the 🗡️ DB Design Challenge — a session obeying the number lands on the wrong section)*. Default when the table is silent: Sonnet for build phases, Opus/Fable for DB/RLS and control phases — **the table always wins over this default** (module 5's table, for example, rules Opus for all five phases). If the current model differs from what the active phase needs, say so before proceeding.

## Read order (rule 4, no memory)
`CLAUDE.md` → `STATUS.md` (which module is active) → 🆕 **the module's approved spec, `docs/specs/module_NN_*/` — if it exists** → `docs/micro_guides/module-N.md` (the whole thing: Live Status Header, step table, Decisions Ledger, the step plan, the self-update protocol — locate sections by heading NAME, the § numbers differ between guides).

🔴 **The approved spec is tier 2 in the truth hierarchy; this micro-guide is tier 5. Where they disagree on something the spec actually covers, the spec wins — per item, not per folder.** ⚠️ **And that is the rule for a step NOT YET BUILT. A conflict that lands on code already written is a change request, not a correction** — take the size-gate below and give Ishay a verdict (`בנה-עכשיו` / `דחה-ל-X`); silently rewriting shipped behaviour to match a document is the failure this whole file exists to prevent. Either way the divergence is reported and logged as a `↳ as-built`/§9 line, never resolved silently in the guide's favour.
**‏`spec.md` §① is the module's own reading list** — it names what to open, in order, **and what NOT to read**. 🔴 **Read the list itself; do not work from a list restated here or anywhere else.** It is longer than it looks *(module 4's runs to 13 numbered sources)*, it names things that live OUTSIDE `docs/specs/` — **the approved mockups in particular sit under `docs/mockups/**/approved/`, not in the spec folder** — and it moves. A restated copy goes stale within days and its reader never knows.

**Resolving the folder — derive it, do not guess it.** Same name as the step guide (`docs/CLAUDE.md` convention): `docs/guides/modules/module_NN_name.md` ⇒ `docs/specs/module_NN_name/`. ⚠️ **The module number is ZERO-PADDED to two digits, and a wrong path fails silently** — it returns nothing, which reads exactly like "this module has no spec". ⇒ **run `ls docs/specs/` and match against what is actually there**, before concluding there is none. **Genuinely no folder ⇒ say so out loud once** ("אין אפיון מאושר למודול הזה — מדריך-המיקרו הוא התוכנית היחידה") **and the micro-guide is the only plan** — the correct state for modules built before the Discovery flow, and it must be a stated finding rather than a silent assumption.
⚠️ **A folder that exists but is INCOMPLETE is a third case, and the dangerous one** — a file `§①` names but which is not on disk. **That is a finding you report to Ishay by name, never a licence to conclude "the spec is silent"** on whatever that file was supposed to answer: silence you could not check is not silence you measured.

## Build loop (behaviour of ⑥2, anchored to the micro-guide + iron rules)

🆕 🔴 **What ⑥2 is, and what it is NOT — assume nothing from it.** ⑥2 is a **per-module** paste-block: it carries that module's **addresses** (its micro-guide, its spec folder, its approved-mockup folder, any hand-computed oracle) and nothing else. **Every RULE lives here, in this file, because this file is the only copy** — a rule written into 13 separate ⑥2 blocks drifts, and that is not a prediction: on 08/08/2026 the same mockup rule was written into a ⑥2 and into this skill hours apart, **and the two contradicted each other within nine hours.** ⇒ **Never treat a ⑥2 as authority on behaviour, never assume it mentions a given rule, and if it states one that conflicts with this file — this file wins, and say so out loud rather than silently picking.** ⚠️ **And ⑥2 does not vary by phase** — it is pasted at every session across DB/RLS, logic, UI, control and QA alike; **what phase you are in comes from the micro-guide's Active step, never from the paste-block.**
- Continue from the **Active step** in the status header.
- **Entering a new phase?** First sweep the Decisions Ledger for OPEN/nod-pending items anchored to this phase's steps and get a consolidated ruling BEFORE the phase's first step (micro-guide §8(h)). ⚠️ **Batch and pre-chew before you take it to him** — one consolidated round, recommendation-first, one line of background per item, full detail only on "פרט לי". A trickle of separate questions across the phase is the failure mode; a long list of decisions tires him, and that is exactly what the sweep exists to prevent.
- **Each build-unit:** post the 🗣️ experience-brief (simple Hebrew: understanding-declaration invited for correction · planned validations · screen/mockup · every mockup-only or spec-silent detail flagged "מהמוקאפ / אפיון-שותק — לאישורך") and **wait for Ishay's approval before writing the unit's code** (a 👤 product-intent gate).
- 🔴 **The mockup shows the STATES, not the state** (Ishay's ruling, rescued 05/08/2026): **empty · error · "unknown" · long text · no permission.** *(Anchor: the "טרם נשלחה" indicator is **tri-state**, and the third state cannot be shown on real data — a single-state mockup would have hidden a third of the screen.)* ⚠️ Not the same as the 🎨 states gate later in the phase: that one checks the **built screen**; this one is about what he approves **before** code exists. And it is what `PROJECT_MASTER §1` means by *"מקרי-קצה הם חלק מהתוצר"* — the room will ask *"ומה קורה אם…"*, and the mockup is where that answer is decided.
- 🆕 🔴 **An APPROVED mockup is not a tier-4 mockup — but it does not win on everything either, and Ishay already ruled exactly where the line falls.** **The test, not a list of paths:** does the module's approved spec **name this mockup as approved**? **No ⇒ tier 4, and everything above applies unchanged.** **Yes ⇒ do not re-draw it and do not re-approve it — and then split by WHAT is in question, per the precedence clause** — **which is a cross-module Discovery ruling, not a module-4 detail: its generic home is `docs/guides/discovery_lessons.md` (grep anchor `המוקאפ גובר על`), and a module whose own `screens-approved.md` restates it is the local override.** 🔴 **Read it at one of those two, not from the summary below** — this is a paraphrase, it is not the SSOT, and it can go stale:
  **‏· APPEARANCE — layout · order · colour · label wording · what is emphasised ⇒ the approved mockup wins. Build it as drawn.**
  **‏· BEHAVIOUR & DATA — what a click does · what is saved · where each number comes from · permissions · states · settings ⇒ the SPEC wins, even against the drawing.**
  **‏· Anything that falls cleanly into neither ⇒ 🛑 STOP and ask Ishay. Do not rule it yourself.**
  🔴 **Why "build it as drawn" alone is actively dangerous, measured 08/08/2026:** all eight of module 4's approved mockups still carry a **"טיוטה" banner in their own HTML**, `spec.md` records that decimals shown in them are stale, and it corrects at least one tag the mockup draws that does not exist. **A rule that says only "as drawn" ships all three.** **What survives unchanged:** re-asking approval on a settled screen is still a failure — it makes Ishay re-decide what he already decided and teaches him his rulings are not recorded.
  ⚠️ **And `אפיון-שותק` is a claim you must earn, not a default — scoped by the KIND of detail, and stated** *(Ishay's ruling 08/08/2026)*: a **screen detail** ⇒ that surface's screen card + its approved mockup · a **process or timing** detail ⇒ the approved processes document · a **number, weight or formula** ⇒ the algorithm/research section the reading list names. **Then write which sources you actually checked, beside the flag.** 🔑 **Why scoped and not "check all of §①": measured 08/08/2026 — module 4's three main spec documents alone are ~2,280 lines, and module 3 had 18 build-units.** Re-reading that per unit is a rule nobody executes, and **a rule people skip silently is worse than a narrower one they actually run.** **The stated-sources line is what keeps it honest** — an absence-claim is only as wide as the search behind it, so the search has to be visible. **Un-verified silence is a gap you did not look for, wearing the label of one you did.** 📍 **The states of an approved surface are answered in its own screen card** — **find that card's states section by reading the card, not by searching for a heading name: the section numbering differs from card to card.**
  🔒 **What this does NOT subtract — the STATES rule above stands in full.** For an approved mockup the states are usually **already answered in its screen card** ⇒ **read them there and present them**, rather than re-deriving them or dropping them. A state the card does not cover is spec-silent by the test above, and goes to Ishay.
- 🚫 **NEVER tell Ishay "an agent checked it, looks fine."** That accelerates approval, which is the exact opposite of the gate's purpose. **Say instead:** *"בדקתי כיווניות וצבעים; מה שלא נבדק הוא אם זה המסך שאתה רוצה."* **His approval is trust, not verification — it never closes your own verification duty.**
- 🔴 **Fan-out cost-check applies to review/verification sweeps too, not only build work — even when it feels urgent.** Dispatching parallel agents beyond ~5-6 to review or sweep several surfaces still needs the pre-estimate-and-ask step; a review task doesn't earn an exception the way a build task doesn't. *(Added 10/08/2026 — skipped once.)*
- 🔴 **"Modeled on / copied from X" is a structural claim, not only a visual one.** When a plan or a build step says a new unit follows an existing dialog/screen's language, check the STRUCTURAL contract too — wrapper elements, default behaviours of the shared components involved — not only that fields and labels look the same. Visual similarity is easy to confirm and easy to mistake for full parity.
- **🎯 The brief MUST end with "מה ייחשב עובד" — 3–5 Hebrew sentences, approved together with the mockup (added 30/07/2026).** Each sentence states **one outcome the user can observe after acting**, with **real values from the real data**. Verification is then written against THIS list, never against my memory of what I built — a test written by the same mind that wrote the code inherits its blind spots.
  🆕 🔴 **And this list is WRITTEN INTO THE MICRO-GUIDE beside its step — not only posted in chat.** *(Added 08/08/2026 after a scan measured it: the rule has existed since 30/07, module 3 was built after it, and `"מה ייחשב עובד"` occurs **once** across all three micro-guides — and that once is a §9 history line, not an output. A rule whose only trace is chat cannot be skipped visibly, and Ishay cannot read the code to notice.)* **Chat is where it gets approved; the guide is where it survives the session.** 🔑 **And each sentence carries where it came from** — `spec.md §✅#N`, another named acceptance section, or `יחידה-ספציפית` — so a session resuming after an interruption can tell a harvested criterion from an invented one, which is impossible once the chat is gone.
  🆕 🔴 **When the approved spec already carries such a chapter, you HARVEST it — you do not author it.** Its **"✅ מה ייחשב עובד"** is Ishay-approved tier-2 text: the unit's list is **the subset of it this unit is responsible for, quoted**, plus anything unit-specific the spec does not cover. ⚠️ **And "the" chapter may not be the only one** — harvest from **every acceptance section the `§①` reading list names** *(an algorithm or research section can carry its own numbered acceptance tests, and module 4's does)*; anchoring to a single filename silently drops the rest — and if a spec sentence has no unit that owns it, say so, because that is a hole in the plan, not a spare sentence. **Any hand-computed oracle in the spec is copied digit-for-digit and never recomputed** *(a spec that states expected outputs computed by hand did so precisely so the implementation could be checked against something it did not produce)*. **Why re-authoring is the dangerous option, not merely the wasteful one:** this list is written into the test **before** the code, so an invented expectation silently becomes the target the implementation is tuned to pass — the bug then survives with a green test sitting on top of it, and the one independent check the module had is gone.
  ⚠️ **Two failure modes, and the second is the likelier one:**
  **(a) Naming a control instead of an outcome.** "יש כפתור X" / "המסך נטען" describe what I built, so they pass while the feature is broken.
  **(b) Vague sentences that satisfy the rule on paper.** "המסך עובד", "הסינון תקין" — a list like that is worse than no list, because it looks done. **The real-values requirement is what prevents this**; a sentence with no concrete number or string is not finished.
  **What it REPLACES (F1):** inventing the verification list after the fact. Not an extra gate — the same gate, written earlier.
  📎 The incident that produced this rule is recorded once, in `docs/micro_guides/module-3.md` §9 (30/07/2026) — **not duplicated here**, because this file is re-read on every build turn of every module while §9 is read only by module 3. **For anything visual, the default is a live HTML mockup with his real data, not a verbal screen description** (added 28/07/2026) — Claude reads structure from markup, Ishay reacts to a picture, and both are better served than by prose. A written description is the fallback when the unit has no visual surface.
- **🔒 A unit with a 🗣️ mockup gets no code written until Ishay approves (added 29/07/2026 — after this gate was breached; wording corrected 21/08/2026 — it was promising an enforcement that does not exist).** The rule above already existed and was still broken: mid-mockup-iteration a file under `src/` was edited before approval, and **Ishay is the one who caught it**. ⚠️ **What this gate actually is: Claude's own discipline, not a tool-level block.** `.claude/settings.json` has no `defaultMode`, and Claude has no tool to put itself into Plan Mode (`grep 'defaultMode\|ExitPlanMode' .claude/` returns nothing) — so "entering Plan Mode" is not something Claude can do on its own, and telling Ishay it is a guarantee would be exactly the false reassurance `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` forbids. **What to actually do:** hold off on every file-write tool for the unit until Ishay's explicit approval lands in chat, same as any other 👤 gate. When posting the brief, say it honestly — e.g. *"עוצר כאן ולא כותב קוד עד שתאשר"* (pausing here, no code until you approve) — **without** claiming a system-enforced "מצב תכנון". **If Ishay wants an actual tool-enforced block:** he can press Shift+Tab himself to put the session into real Plan Mode before approving, or a future `PreToolUse` hook could block writes under `src/` while a 🗣️ brief is pending (not built — this is a note on the option, not an implementation). *(Refines iron rule 2, which exempts routine building from Plan Mode — the exemption assumes an already-approved plan, and a 🗣️ unit is precisely the case where approval does not yet exist.)*
- **Write the test first** for new business logic in `src/lib/` (iron rule 14) — write the unit test, watch it fail for the right reason, then implement until it passes. Not after. A test written after the code tends to confirm what was built rather than catch what was missed; this is especially load-bearing for pricing/Smart-Match formulas, where a wrong test-after can rubber-stamp a wrong calculation.
- 🆕 🌊 **A step is not closed until its ripples are swept AND the sweep is written as a line** *(Ishay's ruling 09/08/2026 — he had to ask for a ripple audit twice in one day, and his words were "מוזר שאני צריך להגיד כל הזמן")*. 🔑 **Why this is a separate bullet and not "iron rule 13 already says so": rule 13 fires on a DECISION that changes schema / a §7 item / a work process. It emits no signal at all when a build step merely FINISHES** — which is where most drift is actually born. **Measured, module 4 step 2.2:** the deviation was logged in §10 exactly as required, and **four** places still went stale — a `[x]` DoD line still reading the old test count, the step's own instruction still stating the approach that was *not* taken (no inline `↳ as-built`), an approved Ledger assumption still naming the superseded mechanism, and the algorithm SSOT still carrying the original formula with nothing pointing at the as-built. ⇒ **walk five targets at every step close: ① the step's `↳ as-built` · ② the deviations log · ③ every DoD checkbox whose number or claim this step moved · ④ the Ledger row the step implements · ⑤ any approved-spec/research section that now describes what you built differently** — and for ⑤ the fix is a **tagged as-built pointer, never an edit to a number** (the spec stays the SSOT). **Then write one line naming what you touched, or the word `אין`** — without it, a session that swept and a session that skipped produce identical output, and Ishay cannot read the code to tell them apart.
- **Verify every 🔻🤖 yourself** with a concrete mechanism + evidence (SQL count / unit / E2E / preview screenshot), report it, and continue. Mid-phase visual evidence is 🤖-with-screenshot, never a human wait.
- **Stop at every 👤 gate and at end-of-phase** (incl. the end-of-Phase-3 🎨 UX & functional review). **Before applying any migration:** the typed-echo gate (Ishay types the migration name — DB protocol); a plain approval is not sufficient to apply, and it is not pre-granted at an earlier step.

Open it and follow it there, in the same session.** *(Verify → refresh `docs/schema.sql` → commit the migration **and** the snapshot together; that file carries the exact steps and the "not approved without it" rule, and it is the SSOT.)* **The trigger stays here on purpose** — a session applying a migration through MCP never opens the migrations folder, so without this line it would never learn the owning file exists. **The steps do not stay here:** they were duplicated verbatim, and two copies of a procedure drift *(this file already produced exactly that failure on 08/08/2026 with a rule split between a paste-block and here)*.

- **The Self-Update Protocol is a checklist to RUN, not background reading.** The micro-guide's own Self-Update Protocol section (locate by heading NAME — the § number differs between guides; already in the Read order above) is the SSOT for what a mid-build change ripples into — its (a)-(d)/(h) are restated verbatim there and (e)-(g) cite CLAUDE.md iron rules 13/15/16; don't duplicate any of it here. Concretely: the moment a step transition, a deviation, a new open question, an applied migration, or a change touching a future module actually happens, stop and match it to its lettered clause **right then** — not only when the guide is first read at session start, and not deferred to end-of-session.

After the third, STOP — report to Ishay in plain Hebrew: what fails, what you tried, and your best hypothesis.
